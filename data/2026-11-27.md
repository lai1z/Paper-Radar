Abstract: Finding valid Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) is difficult for non-linear systems subject to actuation constraints and moving in complex environments. One way to enable analytically derived CBFs is to represent obstacles as supporting halfplanes, allowing the agent to move safely on one side. In this paper, we propose such a CBF for an agent with Dubins car dynamics. A potential drawback of halfplane CBFs is that they can be highly conservative, overestimating the obstacles and thereby restricting the agent's motion. To address this problem, we make the CBF halfplanes less restrictive as follows. First, the orientation of the supporting halfplane for a single obstacle is optimised to find a safe control input as close as possible to the desired one. Second, we use multiple halfplanes to better approximate complex geometries, and derive rules for handling halfplane intersections. Comparative simulations show that our method reduces conservatism compared to existing CBF techniques, enabling tighter obstacle clearance and faster goal arrival, with delays reduced by more than 33%. Finally, experiments on an autonomous hydrofoil boat demonstrate successful real-world implementation.
